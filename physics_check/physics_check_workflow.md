Assumptions: Horizontal bed joints. Stability check under self-weight only. The number of courses follows from the surface height. The number of bricks in course 0 (and in closed courses) follows from the course's own length; each open course above it is laid over the head joints of the course below (running bond), with its ends filled up to the surface edge.
Input: surface, brick dimensions (brickL, brickW, brickH), mortar bed joint thickness mortar_t, running bond offset_distance (closed courses only), material properties (brick density, friction coefficient mu, mortar/glue flexural tensile strength f_t), safety factor SF, nominal head joint head_joint (sets the brick count and joints in step 2), head joint range [min_head, max_head] = [5, 25] mm used when the courses are laid out in step 2 (min_head is also the L0 collision check; max_head is not an L0 check), max overhang per course, max iterations max_iter
Note: confirm brick density (scripts default to 1900 kg/m3; fired clay brick is ~1800-2000 kg/m3). Tension demand scales directly with it.

1. Receive the surface from Rhino.
2. Scale the surface to fit a whole number of bricks, then fit the bricks:
    - Height: scale the surface down in Z (about its base) to the nearest whole number of courses below its current height h:
        n_courses = floor((h + mortar_t) / (brickH + mortar_t))
        target height = n_courses * (brickH + mortar_t) - mortar_t
        Z scale factor = target height / h (<= 1). If n_courses < 2, stop: the surface is too short.
      Do this first, because it moves the heights where the courses are cut.
    - Length (per course, because the plan changes with height): cut the scaled surface at each course's mid-height, z = k * (brickH + mortar_t) + brickH / 2. Measure the length along the brick centreline (offset brickW / 2 from the surface if the surface is the wall face, not the centreline). The section is not stretched: each course keeps the plan of the surface, so neighbouring courses stay where the surface puts them and the overhang between courses is the surface's own.
      (An earlier version stretched every section in Y to a fixed N * (brickL + head_joint). Because each course got its own stretch factor about one shared centre, neighbouring courses slid apart by (sf_k - sf_k-1) * distance from the centre and lost contact, and sections running along X were blown up into S-shaped curls.)
    - Course 0 (open; the layout of baseline.gh), with L = brickL and g = head_joint:
        - Trim the course at both ends by L + g/2. The rest is its middle curve.
        - Middle-brick count m, with middle joint = middle curve / m - L: the m whose joint is closest to g within [min_head, max_head] (as for closed courses below). If no m gives a joint in that range, the largest m with joint >= min_head. If not even one middle brick fits, stop and report the course.
        - A full brick flush with each end, plus m bricks centred in m equal cells of the middle curve (m + 2 bricks). The end joints are g/2 + (cell - L)/2, about g.
    - Open courses above an open course (running bond), laid bottom up, with L = brickL and g = head_joint:
        - Bond bricks: one full brick centred over each head joint of the course below (the joint's midpoint projected onto this course's line), so it bears on the two bricks either side of the joint. Bond bricks are not clipped to the surface edge; one may hang past it by at most half a brick. Joints whose projection lands past the course line's ends are left out.
        - Middle bricks: if the course is longer or shorter than the one below, its head joints would absorb the difference, so middle bricks are inserted (into the widest joint) or removed (the one with the tightest joints), choosing the count whose mean head joint is closest to g while staying >= min_head. The centres are then moved as little as possible (least squares, inserted bricks weighted less) with every head joint in [min_head, max_head], measured as the real gap between the chord footprints.
        - End fill: each gap between the outermost bond brick and the surface edge is packed outward with full bricks while one fits, then a half bat if one fits (each piece needs its length + min_head; joints g, or less down to min_head where tight). A piece counts as fitting if it runs past the edge by at most end_overrun (1 mm). With end_support on (default), each end piece must also bear on the course below by itself (min_contact, min_balance); otherwise the fill is cut back from the edge (a full brick becomes a half bat, or pieces are left out), so the course steps back over an open gap instead of hanging over it. A leftover gap smaller than a half bat stays open; course ends further than end_tol (50 mm) from the edge are reported.
        - If no head joint below falls on the course line, the course is placed like course 0 instead (even courses) or with a half brick (L/2) flush with each end and m + 1 bricks on the cell boundaries (odd courses, trimmed by L + g).
    - Closed courses: a course of n bricks spans n * (brickL + joint), so joint = length / n - brickL. Choose the n whose joint is closest to head_joint within [min_head, max_head]:
        n_max = floor(length / (brickL + min_head)),  n_min = ceil(length / (brickL + max_head))
        n = round(length / (brickL + head_joint)) clamped to [n_min, n_max]; if n_min > n_max, n = n_max.
      If fewer than three bricks fit, stop and report the course. Place the n bricks evenly in a running bond, with odd courses shifted by offset_distance.
    - Curvature: bricks are straight chords, so on a curve they lose head joint on the concave side. If a course collides, first redistribute its head joints so every joint is in [min_head, max_head]. If that cannot work on a running-bond course, the course is left as it is and the L0 repair resolves the collision (no brick is dropped, so the bond over the joints below is kept). On course 0 or a closed course, drop one middle brick (one brick on a closed course) and lay the course out again, so its ends stay in line with the courses above and below. Its head joints may then exceed max_head; this is reported, not limited (capping them would shorten the course and leave the bricks above its ends without support). The settled positions are the fitted reference.
    - The bed face of course k sits exactly at z = k * (brickH + mortar_t) (brick centre at + brickH / 2), so the bed faces are coplanar (CRA only detects interfaces within tmax = 1e-5 m). Each brick is a frame (centre + rotation following the course tangent). This fitted position is the reference for the per-brick deviation reported in the output. The generated wall is the subject of analysis.
3. (L0) Geometry check, per brick:
    - Contact: the total plan overlap of each brick with the bricks below is at least min_contact times the brick's own bed area (length * brickW; a half brick uses brickL/2). It is checked as a total, not per brick below.
    - Balance: each brick's centre lies at least min_balance (10 mm) inside its contact region with the course below - the extent of all its contact polygons together, measured along the brick's own axes (along the course and across the wall). A brick bridging a joint is measured across both supports and passes; a brick resting on the end of a single brick fails, however large its contact area, because it would tip off on its own. Across the wall this is stricter than max_overhang: the centre must stay min_balance inside the support's face, so the effective out-of-plane step is at most brickW/2 - min_balance per course.
    - Collision: bricks in the same course keep a head joint of at least min_head. Check every pair in the course, not only neighbours (non-adjacent bricks can collide on tight concave curves). Bricks in different courses cannot collide because of the horizontal bed joints.
    - Design limit: each brick's overhang relative to the brick(s) below stays within the max overhang. It is checked on the placed bricks, because chord placement and the running bond differ slightly from the surface. There is no limit on the total wall thickness (the lean added up over the courses); L1 checks that the wall is stable.
4. If L0 fails, modify the bricks (not the surface) with a sequential quadratic program (pc/l0qp.py) that moves all bricks together.
    - Each brick may move along its course, sideways, and rotate in plan about its centre (up to max_rotation = 30 deg from the course direction). The objective is a least-squares sum of: each brick's deviation from its target; qp_smooth (1) x the difference in sideways deviation between neighbouring bricks of a course, so a deviation spreads along the course instead of jumping; and (brickL/2)^2 x the difference between each brick's rotation and the local slope of the sideways deviation, so bricks turn to follow the deviated course line instead of leaving a staircase of protruding corners.
    - Find the layout closest to each brick's target position (least squares of the plan moves) that passes every contact, overhang and collision check. After the fit only the brick geometry counts: the bricks may depart from the surface as far as the checks require (max_move = 0, the default, means no limit; a positive max_move caps the deviation from the fitted position). The target is the fitted position, or where L1 last moved the brick, so L0 does not undo an L1 repair. Course 0, the base of the wall, does not move. The end bricks of open courses do not move along the course, so the wall ends follow the surface edge.
    - The overlap areas are piecewise linear, so solve a sequence of quadratic programs, each linearised at the current layout and limited to moves of at most R (starting at qp_trust = 20 mm). Re-check the exact geometry after each; keep the round if the total violation went down and no brick lost all its support, and double R (up to 4 x qp_trust) if the round's largest move reached R; otherwise undo it and halve R. Stop when L0 passes, after qp_max_iter (50) rounds, or when R is below min_step.
    - Checks that already fail may be violated in the QP at a high cost per mm, so it always has a solution.
    - When the QP stalls, clean up the sub-mm leftovers of the linearisation: for each failing brick, the smallest single move that makes it pass (exact geometry, searched in move_step steps), kept only if it lowers the violation of the bricks around it.
    - Bricks that still fail contact have no position with enough support: drop up to max_drop of them, lowest course first, and re-solve so the bricks above adjust to the gap.
    - A brick that still fails at the end has no passing position the QP could find: flag it.
5. (L1) Only when L0 passes: if bricks still fail L0 after step 4 (flagged), stop and report them without running L1, because CRA on a geometrically invalid wall rarely converges. Otherwise export brick frames to JSON (export_frames_json.py) and run CRA_penalty with course 0 as fixed supports (cra_check.py --penalty, with mu and density from the inputs). Bridge Rhino <-> CPython with Hops, rhino.compute or a subprocess call so the loop runs without manual steps. Limit each solve to cra_timeout seconds (the solve runs in a child process that is killed at the limit); if it is hit, stop the loop and report L1 as not checked.
6. Read the CRA result:
    - No tension needed -> the wall is stable as a dry stack. Done.
    - Tension needed -> highlight the bricks/interfaces that need tension. For each tensioned interface, compute the net tensile stress from the CRA corner forces:
        - resultant normal force P = sum of the corner normal forces
        - moment M about the interface centroid from the corner forces
        - sigma_t = M/Z - P/A, using the actual contact polygon's area A and section modulus Z
        - if sigma_t > f_t / SF -> move the brick above the failing interface in its X-Y plane to the closest position that passes, within the max overhang. Work top-down. If no position within the limits passes, flag the brick.
        - Note: the penalty solve finds the least tension needed for equilibrium, not the elastic stress distribution, so sigma_t is a lower-bound estimate; SF covers this.
    - Non-convergence -> diagnose before moving anything:
        - check the interface count and look for isolated/floating blocks (geometry problem -> back to step 3)
        - otherwise it is likely sliding, which tension cannot resist -> report the bricks involved; fix by reducing the overhang of the sliding bricks within the limits, then re-check tension.
7. Outer loop: after any move in step 6, go back to step 3 (re-run L0, then L1). Stop when L0 and L1 both pass, or after max_iter iterations. If max_iter is reached, report the remaining failing bricks/interfaces and the flagged bricks.

Output: a frame (centre + rotation) for each brick, the number of bricks and the head joint of each course, plus per-brick status (passed / moved / flagged / failed = still failing when the loop stopped), each brick's distance from its fitted position (how far the checks changed the design; reported, not limited), and the per-interface sigma_t / (f_t / SF). This is fed into Rhino to generate the updated structure for visualization.
